The FAO-56 Water Balance

A daily dual crop coefficient soil water balance, implemented strictly from Allen et al. (1998) — every equation carries its number from the manual. The engine keeps three soil compartments: an evaporation layer, the root zone, and the subsoil below it. This page shows how those layers move as a crop grows and a fallow follows, then documents each formula against the chapter it comes from.

The three compartments over a season

Depth runs downward; time runs left to right. As the crop roots deepen, the boundary between the root zone and the subsoil drops with it — then springs back to the minimum when the crop is removed and a bare fallow begins.

How the depths are counted

Each state variable is the water held over a depth range. Two of them start at the surface and overlap: the evaporation layer is the top slice of the root zone.

The equations, chapter by chapter

Everything below is Allen et al. (1998). Symbols: θFC/θWP field capacity / wilting point (m³ m⁻³), ETo reference ET, u2 wind at 2 m, RHmin minimum relative humidity, h canopy height. A trailing ⁻ means yesterday's value.

Chapter 7 · Crop coefficients & canopy
Table 17
Basal crop coefficient Kcb
PART 1
Kcb(t): flat at Kcb,ini → rising → Kcb,mid → declining → Kcb,end

The trapezoid over the four growth stages (initial, development, mid, late). Root depth Zr grows alongside it, from the minimum at planting to the crop's maximum.

Eq. 72
Upper limit Kc_max
PART 1
Kc,max = max{ 1.2 + [0.04(u2−2) − 0.004(RHmin−45)] (h/3)0.3 , Kcb+0.05 }

The ceiling on evaporation + transpiration, corrected for the day's wind and humidity over the standing canopy.

Eq. 76
Fraction of ground covered fc
PART 1
fc = [ (Kcb−Kc,min) / (Kc,max−Kc,min) ]1+0.5h

Drives the exposed-and-wetted fraction few below. Capped at 0.99.

Chapter 7 · Soil evaporation (surface layer, book De)
Eq. 73
Evaporable water TEW, REW
STEP 3
TEW = 1000 (θFC − 0.5θWP) Ze    REW = REWfrac · TEW

The surface layer's water dries all the way to air-dry (half of wilting point) — that ½θWP floor is what distinguishes it from the root zone.

Eq. 74
Evaporation reduction Kr
STEP 3
Kr = 1  if  De⁻ ≤ REW   else   (TEW − De⁻) / (TEW − REW)

Full-rate evaporation until the readily-evaporable water is gone, then a linear taper to zero at air-dry.

Eq. 71 · 75
Evaporation coefficient Ke & flux E
STEP 3
few = min(1−fc, fw)    Ke = min[ Kr(Kc,max−Kcb), fewKc,max ]    E = Ke·ETo

Evaporation is limited both by how dry the surface is (Kr) and by how much soil is exposed and wet (few).

Chapter 8 · Transpiration & water stress (root zone, book Dr)
Eq. 82
Total available water TAW
STEP 4
TAW = 1000 (θFC − θWP) Zr

The plant-available water in the root zone. Its floor is the wilting point θWP — roots cannot extract below it. Grows as Zr deepens.

Eq. 83
Depletion fraction p & readily-available water RAW
STEP 4
p = clamp( ptab + 0.04(5 − ETc), 0.1, 0.8 )    RAW = p·TAW

Table 22's no-stress depletion, adjusted for the day's demand.

Eq. 84
Water-stress coefficient Ks
STEP 4
Ks = 1  if  Dr⁻ < RAW   else   (TAW − Dr⁻) / (TAW − RAW)

The only thing that reduces transpiration. No demand-vs-supply rescaling is applied anywhere.

Eq. 80
Transpiration T & crop ET
STEP 4
T = Ks·Kcb·ETo    ETc = T + E

When Kcb = 0 (fallow) transpiration is exactly zero — the profile then loses water only through E.

Chapter 8 · The daily water balance
SCS-CN
Runoff RO — one departure from the manual
STEP 2
S = 25400/CN − 254    RO = (P − 0.2S)² / (P + 0.8S)

FAO-56 leaves runoff to the caller; DualKc Studio supplies the SCS Curve Number method so a weather file alone is enough.

Eq. 74
Surface depletion De
STEP 8
De = De⁻ − (P−RO) − I/fw + E/few + DPe ,   bounded [0, TEW]

The evaporation layer's running balance, capped at TEW (air-dry).

Eq. 85
Root-zone depletion Dr
STEP 9
Dr = Dr⁻ − (P−RO) − I + ETc + DPr ,   bounded [0, TAW]

The full ETc — transpiration and evaporation — depletes the root zone. Capped at TAW (wilting point).

Eq. 86
Deep percolation DP
STEP 9
DPr = max[ (P−RO) + I − ETc − Dr⁻ , 0 ]

Water beyond field capacity drains downward — here, into the tracked subsoil.

FAO-33 · Water-limited yield (Doorenbos & Kassam, 1979)
FAO-33
Yield penalty — on transpiration, not ET
curves.js
1 − Y/Ymax = Ky ( 1 − T / Tx ) ,   Tx = Kcb·ETo

The water–yield production function. This engine applies it to the ratio of actual to potential transpiration (T / Tx, with Tx = Kcb·ETo) — not the original ETa/ETx — so the penalty reflects water stress on the crop's transpiration, not on soil evaporation. Evaluated per crop window (never averaged across a rotation), and returned as null, not zero, when the crop has no attainable yield Ymax defined.

The one thing beyond the manual

FAO-56 does not define the water content of the soil below the root zone — implementations usually assume it sits at field capacity. DualKc Studio tracks it.

The subsoil (Zr→Z_profile) carries its actual water as a third store, Ss. As roots deepen, the newly reached slice hands its real water to the root zone — so a crop can find water banked during a previous fallow instead of always meeting field capacity. Water percolating past the roots enters Ss; whatever it cannot hold at field capacity leaves the profile as deep percolation. Everything else is strictly the manual.

A practical minimum root depth. The root zone is never allowed to collapse to the evaporation layer: it is held at Ze + 0.2 m at planting and retained through fallow. A seedling responds over a real layer, not an infinitesimal one; this also keeps Ks well-posed and gives the fallow a thin store to carry moisture into the next crop. This mirrors the minimum effective rooting depth used by models such as AquaCrop.
